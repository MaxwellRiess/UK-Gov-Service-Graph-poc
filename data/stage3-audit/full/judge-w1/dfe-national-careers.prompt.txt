Get Britain Working: an update on the Jobs and Careers Service Ministerial foreword The point of a Jobcentre should be getting people into good jobs. Work offers more than a wage. It gives us a sense of pride, purpose and identity – as well as being positive for physical and mental health. But too many people, particularly young people, are being let down – not getting the right support, signed off, and abandoned to a life on benefits. The human consequences can be lifelong and devastating for an individual. Employers and the country suffer too, through lost talent and contributions. Our reforms are about more actively helping people to change the story of their lives – putting work, skills and opportunity at the heart of everything we do – especially for the more than 1 million young people not in education, employment or training. We cannot afford to write off a generation when our population is getting older, with a higher prevalence of illness and disability. The world is changing rapidly. New technologies, including AI , are transforming the world of work and the skills employers need. But this government inherited an employment support system which has not kept pace and is not helping enough people into work. In a typical month, just 8% of those on Universal Credit looking for work secure a job. Even worse, it’s half that for people who have been unemployed for over a year, meaning the system is particularly struggling to deliver for those who need it the most. This is a huge waste of potential, puts pressure on public services, limits productivity and acts as a drag on economic growth. People’s lives are complex, and they often face different combinations of barriers to work. Yet work, health and skills support have not been joined up enough. And a one-size-fits-all approach has failed to offer help that is personalised enough or to set high ambitions for people seeking work. The system has not only failed to deliver for people, but for businesses too, with insufficient focus on understanding employers’ needs and matching the right people to the right opportunities. This has eroded employers’ confidence in Jobcentre Plus and in the quality of candidates the service sends their way. In turn, this has further limited the opportunities people can access through Jobcentres. Work coaches and other colleagues in local communities right across the country have huge amounts of expertise and experience. But they are consistently held back by the system. A system which prioritises processes over employment outcomes, and which is too bogged down by bureaucracy. This means our people do not have the right tools, training or time to provide high-quality employment support that helps people find the right jobs. The best work coaches help people in spite of the system, rarely because of it. To address these challenges, our Get Britain Working White Paper set out a fundamentally different approach, with less admin and more ambition. We are investing in supporting people into work, putting skills and careers at the heart of a new Jobs and Careers Service. We are creating a service built around results, not processes, with outcomes focused on getting more people into work and to support them making progress in their careers. It will be clear about the help people can expect and what is expected in return. It will work closely with employers to help them find people with the right skills to fill real vacancies, providing clear routes off benefits and into sustained employment. And it will maximise the support we offer by being digital where possible, and human where needed. The strength of the service lies in its people and their professional judgement. We will invest in leadership, skills and learning to give our staff the tools, confidence and accountability to focus on what matters most: jobs, progression and results. The new Jobs and Careers Service will better match people to the right roles, support employers to access the skills they need, and build a more productive and prosperous country. This report sets out how we are turning that ambition into action. Creating a more proactive system designed to unlock people’s potential, by always asking that fundamental question: “How can we help you find a job?” The Rt Hon Dame Diana Johnson DBE MP, Minister for Employment Introduction 1. As this government set out in its manifesto, everyone should have the opportunity to build a better future – to find secure, meaningful work, develop new skills and progress in their career. Our public employment service must do more to drive high participation in the labour market, with clear opportunities for progression into sustained employment. This is because we know good work supports health, wellbeing and higher living standards for individuals, and helps local communities thrive, driving economic growth. 2. Jobcentre Plus benefits from its work coaches’ expertise and dedication. They are rooted in the communities they serve and committed to helping customers find work across Great Britain. This is borne out by the high levels of customer satisfaction with the support they receive. [footnote 1] 3. However, sustained employment and progression remain too low. The current system, introduced in 2002, still reflects the labour market needs and technologies of that time and has not effectively kept pace with our evolving labour market. New industries, demographic changes and technological advances offer new opportunities and reinforce the need to refocus our services. This will ensure that Jobcentre Plus can deliver personalised, progression-focused support to meet our ambitions to Get Britain Working. 4. This gap between ambition and delivery requires a fundamental change in how employment support is designed and delivered. That is why the UK Government committed to creating a new Jobs and Careers Service: a modern, integrated, digitally enabled employment service that brings together Jobcentre Plus and the National Careers Service in England, and works hand in hand with employers and local partners in the wider employment support system. 5. As set out in the Get Britain Working White Paper, our aim is clear: to increase engagement, employment and earnings. This means helping more economically inactive people to engage with work, often through specialist, local provision; supporting people into quality, sustained employment; and increasing earnings by enabling career progression. We will do this by setting high ambitions and clear expectations for customers, recognising their potential and addressing barriers to work. We will also set high expectations for our employees, underpinned by improved training and shared accountability. 6. This report builds on the Get Britain Working White Paper and provides further detail on the new service, following our commitment to the Work and Pensions Select Committee in autumn 2025. It is an interim update, and we will publish further evidence and detail on the service’s design and implementation in the coming months. The report sets out how we will put our ambitions into practice for those who use our services: Our customers will have a clear understanding of our expectations and ambitions for them, with access to tailored employment, skills and careers support to find and progress in work. We will deliver this through new digital services and more targeted support. Employers and local places will receive more joined-up support and improved matching of customers to opportunities that meet local labour market needs and growth priorities. We will deliver this through stronger labour market data and deeper partnerships with employers and places across Great Britain. Our people will provide consistently high-quality support focused on getting people into work rather than just following processes. We will deliver this by giving work coaches across Great Britain, and careers advisers in England, better tools, data and training. Leaders will hold their teams accountable through new, outcome-focused performance frameworks. 7. In each chapter, we set out the progress already underway and the changes we will deliver by the end of this Parliament. These are targeted to have the greatest impact on sustained employment and higher earnings through career progression. 8. The new service will be rooted in evidence, with evaluation central to design and delivery. Annexes provide further detail on our evidence base and our evaluation strategy. 9. DWP runs Jobcentre Plus and wider employment support across Great Britain, operating within a broader system that includes local government, health services, skills providers, employers and voluntary and community organisations, as well as the Scottish and Welsh Governments. DWP also runs adult skills and careers services in England. The Scottish and Welsh Governments deliver their own adult skills and careers services, and some employment support. This means elements of our service will look different in different places, but our ambitions remain the same. We will work closely with the Scottish and Welsh Governments to deliver consistently high-quality support and help all our customers find work, wherever they live. Problem Statement: the current system 1. The current employment support system was designed for past labour market conditions. It is based on evidence that frequent face-to-face appointments help people to move into sustained work quickly. [footnote 2] [footnote 3] While this was appropriate at the time, it no longer reflects today’s labour market or the modern technology that underpins other public services. 2. Since the introduction of Jobcentre Plus, the labour market has evolved significantly. Profound economic, technological and demographic change has transformed how people live, learn and work, creating both more complex barriers to employment and new opportunities to support customers. Emerging industries, changing employer needs and the growing importance of skills and in‑work progression require more flexible, personalised employment support. The current service has not kept pace with these changes – our new service must evolve with the labour market. 3. Employment support is delivered by a dedicated, motivated workforce. However, work coaches lack the support and tools to make the most effective use of their skills, experience and professional judgement. Too much Jobcentre Plus capacity is concentrated on a relatively narrow group of customers, while many others receive little or no meaningful help to enter work or progress once there. Large amounts of time can be absorbed by managing benefits processes and appointments. 4. Both work coaches and their customers want to build collaborative, employment‑focused relationships. However, the scope for personalised, action-focused support that helps people into work has been constrained by a lack of time. [footnote 4] 5. As a result, many customers remain trapped in low-paid work or reliant on the benefits system, rather than moving into jobs that match their skills or career ambitions. This is supported by evidence which shows that only 2 in 5 low-paid people in 2017 were consistently earning enough by 2023 to no longer be considered low-paid. [footnote 5] Fragmented local services, and the lack of a consistent framework to align the work, health and skills system at a local level, make it harder for people to find the right opportunities. 6. The current system does not work for employers either, as many do not engage with any Jobcentre Plus support. [footnote 6] Employers who do use our services often report being sent candidates who are not job-ready or a good match for the role. This exacerbates a skills gap problem, where employers struggle to identify people with suitable experience and qualifications for their vacancies: over a quarter of all vacancies (27%) in 2024 were due to skills shortages. [footnote 7] This mismatch can lead to significant employment gaps, even in growth sectors with strong demand for entry-level roles. 7. The consequences of these problems are system-wide, placing pressure on public services and limiting economic growth. Individuals struggle to access the right support and opportunities, affecting their wellbeing and financial security. Employers face persistent skills shortages and difficulty filling vacancies, and communities experience weaker labour market outcomes. Chapter 1: Our Customers Our vision for the future service 1. The service will focus on improving sustained employment and career progression for our customers. Reflecting technological advances, it will be digital where possible and human where needed. It will be accessible through multiple channels, targeted at those who need it most and personalised to individual needs. It will be clear what support people will receive, and what customers receiving benefits are expected to do. Whether someone is switching careers, returning to work after illness or planning their next step, they will be able to access the right support at the right time. We will support more people, focusing on those who need it most 2. To increase long-term employment, we will reach those who are not currently receiving regular support from Jobcentre Plus and those who are not on benefits. We will also better target our support by tailoring it to people’s needs: Digital support will be the first port of call for those who are job-ready, and available to everyone across Great Britain. Bespoke work coach employment support will focus on customers who are receiving benefits, are able to work and face greater barriers to work. In England, our integrated careers services will continue to provide high-quality, impartial careers information and advice to everyone. Qualified careers advisers will focus on providing in-depth advice and guidance to customers who face greater barriers to working or learning, regardless of whether they are claiming benefits. In Scotland and Wales, people will continue to have access to the devolved careers services.
