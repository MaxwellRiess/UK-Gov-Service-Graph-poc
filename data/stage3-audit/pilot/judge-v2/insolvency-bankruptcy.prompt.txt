The same test applies: use one for a claim only if it is about this service, in the part of the UK the service covers, or states a general rule that plainly applies to it. A page about a similar service, or another nation's version, does not support the claim.

A page from one local council speaks only for that council. Use it for a claim about a service councils run locally only if it states a rule that applies everywhere (a law, a national scheme, "all councils must"); a council's own phone number, form or document list shows what that council does, so the claim is at most partly_supported from it.

For a field marked "(split into claims)", split it into single factual statements and judge each. Together the claims must cover every factual statement in the field: do not skip one because it is hard to judge. For any other field, return it as one claim.

- supported: the text states it, including every figure, age, date and condition it names. Paraphrase is fine, and so is anything the text plainly implies.
- partly_supported: the text states some of it, but a figure, condition or detail is not in the text.
- contradicted: the claim and the text cannot both be true: a different figure, a route or method the text says does not exist, a sign-in the text names differently. A detail the text leaves out is partly_supported, not contradicted.
- not_stated: the text does not address it.

A claim that you can apply or do this a certain way (online, by phone, by post, in person) is supported only where the text says you can apply, ask or start the service that way. How the service itself is delivered (an assessment held face-to-face, a payment made by post) does not count.

Also list every way the text says you can apply (methods_on_page), and the sign-in it names for applying online, if any (sign_in_on_page).

The verdict and the note must agree. Quotes must be copied exactly from the source text, not paraphrased or stitched together.

Apply for bankruptcy Use this service to apply for bankruptcy in England and Wales. The process is different if you’re applying for bankruptcy in Scotland or applying for bankruptcy in Northern Ireland . You can only apply for bankruptcy online. It costs £680. Before applying, check other options for dealing with debt and find out what happens when you become bankrupt . Start now Before you start You’ll need to provide information about your income, outgoings and debts, including: wage slips benefits or pension statements bills - for example, electricity, credit card and council tax letters from a bailiff or enforcement agent You’ll be asked to create an account. You can start your application and come back to it later by signing in to your account . You will not be able to submit it until you’ve paid the full fee. If you’ve applied for a person at risk of violence (PARV) order , do not submit your application until the court has made a decision about your PARV. It usually takes up to 28 days to get a decision on your application. It can take longer if the ‘adjudicator’ (the person who reviews your application) needs to ask you more questions. Paying the fee You cannot submit your application until you’ve paid the full fee. You can pay in instalments if you cannot afford to pay it all at once. If you are struggling to pay the fee, contact a debt adviser about charities that may be able to help. If you need help with your application Get help from a debt adviser . You can also contact the Insolvency Service helpline.
